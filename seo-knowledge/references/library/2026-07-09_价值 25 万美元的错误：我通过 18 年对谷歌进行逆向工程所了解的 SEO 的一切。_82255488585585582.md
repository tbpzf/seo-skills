Search Was Never A Meritocracy
"Reputation is an idle and most false imposition; oft got without merit, and lost without deserving." - William Shakespeare.

Google has spent the past two decades pushing the narrative that good content, expertise, effort and following the guidelines alone will get you traffic in return.

If that were true, then the best page would consistently outrank every inferior alternative, the most knowledgeable expert would always beat the largest publisher, and a local specialist who spent over a decade researching a subject would never lose to a press release written by AI.

But that's exactly what happens...

Every single day, in every niche, in every country Google operates in.

The industry will tell you that you lost because the algorithm is impossibly sophisticated, machine learning driven and worked on by the brightest PhD-backed engineers in the world.

Some of that is true, but the reality is we didn't have context against that until very recently - When the algorithm itself got exposed, and Google's monopoly forced them to reveal internal secrets to the public.

The FIVE Core Frameworks I Developed Over Eighteen Years

There are dozens of other, individual techniques and strategies that I have either personally developed or use, but these are the core five frameworks that make up my overall philosophy for most campaigns. 

Entity Stacking

Google, and most AI models, now have a real world knowledge base of entities - Your goal is to get your entities (Company name/s, the people at the top of the organization, events, locations etc etc) all recognized, and more importantly, TRUSTED.

The traditional search algo, and AI alike, has no way to physically validate if an entity is real or not, so it has to use a variety of trusted sources. 

As SEOs, we can reverse engineer and build those trusted sources.

I did a pretty popular video breakdown of this entire strategy here -

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4wwnMx86bWA 

Most of the sites you'd need are fairly obvious (Social profiles, directories, review sites, citations, references, local news, press releases etc) and according to @jasonmbarnard you need at least 30 consistent, corroborated links (or mentions) from trusted reference domains. 

Reverse Engineering

Once you've got your entity recognized by Google, EVERY page you build with the intention of ranking and getting organic traffic should be based on what is already happening in the SERP.

Too many SEOs skip this step, and it shows.

Without seeing what is already showing, you don't know if you are cannibalizing a page, blocked by SERP features or even creating the right intent page in the first place.

Load up every keyword you want to target your page at (using a VPN and incognito + mobile versions if needed) then check:

Is there an AI Overview? IF SO: What does it say, what pages are featured, how is it formatted, does it change massively on refresh?
What other SERP features does it have? Map pack, People Also Ask, Image Box, Shopping, Video Feed, News Results etc?
What page is ranking #1? Sites like Wikipedia are very difficult to outrank, but an AI generated article on a DR12 domain is probably very easy...
Do the organic results have features like sitelinks, publish date, favicon etc?

And then you want to reverse engineer the overall root authority of the competitor sites, and the link profiles of the individual pages too:  

Does the page have 100s of quality, niche referring domains? Then it's going to take a while to outrank, especially if it's been there a while.

Consensus SEO

One of the most repeated pieces of advice in the SEO industry is to "create unique content."

The problem is, Google baked consensus into the algorithm.

The algo doesn't just want you to match the predetermined intent it has for the SERP (not user intent - The intent the algo has decided that keyword deserves), it wants you to match what is already there...

The format, the depth, the entities covered, the questions answered. Everything ranking on page 1 is Google showing you its answer key.

Your job isn't to be different. 

Your job is to match the consensus, then beat it on optimization.

When I break down a SERP for consensus, I'm extracting:

Page Type & Format - Are the top 10 listicles? Comparison tables? EComm categories? Calculators or tools? If 8 of 10 results are "10 Best" listicles and you publish a 4,000 word how to guide, you haven't created better content, you've created the wrong page.
Entity & Sub-topic Coverage - What entities, topics and sub-topics does every ranking page cover? That overlap is what the algo expects from new pages entering.
Depth & Structure - Word count ranges, heading patterns, tables, schema. Match the range. A 900-word page doesn't beat a 3,000-word consensus, and a 10,000-word page doesn't either - It just dilutes your targeting.

BUT, pure cloning caps you at "as good as." And Google has patents around information gain - Scoring whether a page adds anything to what the SERP already contains. 

So the winning formula isn't 100% consensus.. It's now roughly 80-90% consensus + 10-20% genuine information gain:

A proprietary stat or data point nobody else has.
Unique entities (New persona, company, tool etc)
An updated figure everyone else has wrong or outdated.
One table, tool or comparison the SERP is missing.
An answer to a clustered question no ranking page actually addresses.

Small amounts, but deliberately placed. 

Too much uniqueness and you've broken intent-match; too little and you're a duplicate the machine has no reason to promote over the incumbent.

And this matters double for AI Overviews... The model synthesizes an answer from the consensus, then cites the sources that corroborate it - So a page that disagrees with the AIO's answer almost never gets cited into it.

The algo doesn't reward unique creativity, it rewards conformity with a measurable edge.

Parasite SEO

Parasite SEO is using high authority domains (paid or built) to rapidly rank for terms you usually wouldn't be able to touch using your own site, or to reinforce existing rankings and citations. 

The algorithm has been tuned to favour root authority and UGC signals so aggressively that they beat out nearly everything else: Content quality, expertise, relevance, history.. all of it.

This is the reason the press release at the beginning ranked so well - Engineers changed the weighting during an update to reward the exact signals that Reuters domain was built towards.

AI SEO

Every few weeks the industry invents a new acronym for this - GEO, AEO, AIO, LLMO.. They're all SEO disciplines that already existed, just shiny new names to try and ride the hype.

The machines are changing, but they inherited the exact same weakness.

ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews, Perplexity - None of them can verify whether anything is true or whether any entity is real. 

So they do precisely what Google's algorithm has always done: They count trusted sources and look for agreement.

An LLM decides what's "true" through corroboration - The same claim appearing across multiple sources.

It decides who you are through co-occurrence - Your entity showing up next to your category, consistently, across the platforms it was trained on and grounds against. 

Consensus isn't just a Google ranking mechanic anymore... It's literally how the models construct reality.

Which is why, if you've been paying attention, you'll notice something about the "new" discipline of AI SEO: It's my same four frameworks above, just wired slightly differently. 

Entity Stacking - The models need to recognize and trust WHO you are before they'll ever cite you. Same 30+ corroborated sources, now feeding training data and retrieval instead of just the Knowledge Graph.
Reverse Engineering - Run your prompts across models, pick up co-occurring sources and entities to optimize for. 
Consensus - Agree with the synthesized answer, structure your chunks for extraction, then add the information gain that makes YOUR version the quotable one.
Parasite SEO - Look at what actually gets cited: Reddit, LinkedIn, YouTube, Wikipedia, press releases, news... The models ground on high-authority platforms. Parasite SEO didn't die in the AI era, it became the new (and best) distribution layer of it.

The one genuinely new rule: AI results are probabilistic, not fixed - There is no position #1 anymore... 

Fewer clicks, yes, but a citation inside an AI answer is something a blue link never was: The machine (and the brand, like Google) itself vouching for you, at the exact moment of the question. 

That's a new positioning for the user journey we never had before.

I didn't build these for the AI era, my core four frameworks I already had walked straight into it.

That's not a coincidence, that's the convergence.

The Convergence

Like I said earlier, individually these frameworks are very powerful, but it's when you bring all 5 together that your campaigns start to print money...

Get your domain and entity validated + trusted by Google, and the other frontier AI models.
Reverse engineer and topical map every keyword you want to rank for and generate traffic from.
Create your individual pages to match the correct SERP and competitor consensus, with small additions for info gain. 
Generate capital from harder to rank for keywords with Parasite SEO, and reinforce existing rankings and visibility with more pages.
Map your existing AI visibility, plug the holes where necessary, change targeting where it makes sense and make sure your campaigns overlap for maximum ROI.

Then stack improvements and expansions with the more data you collect.

And this is why my campaigns "out-ROI" everyone else in SEO...

Every framework de-risks the one after it:

The entity work means your new pages aren't entering the SERP as a stranger. 

The reverse engineering means you never build the wrong page, chase the wrong keyword, or pick a fight with a competitor you can't beat. 

Consensus means every page enters pre-matched to what the algo already rewards. 

Parasites mean you're generating revenue from week one instead of month nine - Funding the campaign with the campaign!

And the AI visibility layer means every asset you build is now working across two surfaces at once, because the models are grounding on the same entities, the same consensus, and the same platforms you've already stacked for traditional organic.

What Happens by 2030?

Every interview, every conference Q&A, every podcast I do, I get asked for predictions on where I see the industry going.

And to be honest, it's very difficult because it's based almost entirely on decisions made by big tech and various government bodies.

Copywriters and graphic designers were the first to really feel the wrath of an AI future, but SEO is affected on almost every layer - Search itself, the processes, the tools, the guidelines, everything is changing rapidly.

Google isn't going anywhere - There's $200 BILLION+ of ad revenue making sure of it, but the click-through economy underneath it keeps shrinking as answers get synthesized on the surface.

I personally believe that Google (and most of the big platforms) are still trying to move to a zero-click environment - Attempting to keep users inside their respective ecosystems as much as possible.

As a result, some form of AI Mode/AI Overviews will become the default search experience - I don't think it looks like what it does now, but I believe that happens in the next 6-24 months.

So guideline-followers become a commodity - Their playbook is public, so it's exactly what AI automates first. 

Reverse engineers become more valuable than they've ever been, because in a world of multiple opaque, probabilistic machines, the only durable skill is measuring what they reward faster than they change.

Then AI agents start researching, comparing and buying on behalf of their users. Which means pages optimized for machine consumption (Clean structure, extractable data, feeds etc...) will convert customers who never even saw your website, or potentially even your logo.

And none of these predictions requires my frameworks to change, that's on purpose. Frameworks built on rules break when the rules change. Frameworks built on measurement don't care.
