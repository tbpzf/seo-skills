Google Search Console is filtering out roughly 90% of the keywords your site actually ranks for, and I can prove it.

I ran a side-by-side test and found 10.8x more keywords in Bing Webmaster Tools than GSC showed me. Today, I'm giving you the free Claude prompt that turns one Bing CSV into a full keyword picture, because you deserve to see what Google is hiding.
I tested this with a legacy client’s site (TheMatchArtist.com). Same site, same 12-month window, both tools pulling search performance data.


The results:
Google Search Console: 657 unique keywords
Bing Webmaster Tools: 7,081 unique keywords
That's 10.8x more keywords visible in Bing than Google shows you.


And the overlap? Almost zero. Only 343 keywords appeared in both exports. That means Bing is surfacing 6740 keywords that Google completely hides from you in GSC.


Let that sink in. Google is filtering, anonymizing, and suppressing the long-tail queries your site is actually ranking for. Bing doesn't do this. It shows you everything.
Why This Matters
If you're only using GSC for keyword research, you're working with about 10% of the picture.


All those long-tail, question-based, and niche queries that drive real traffic? Google hides them. Bing shows them.


This is especially useful for:
Discovering content gaps you didn't know existed
Finding long-tail keywords to build new pages around
Understanding the actual breadth of queries your site serves
Feeding better data into your content strategy
How It Works
I built a Claude prompt that does everything for you. You upload your 12 months Bing Webmaster Tools Keyword Report CSV and the prompt handles the rest.


Here's what it does:
1. Fetches the live multiplier. The prompt goes to StatCounter and grabs Bing's current worldwide market share. As of January 2026, Bing holds 4.45% of all search. It calculates the multiplier: 100 / 4.45 = 22.47x. Think of it like this: Bing is a small window into total search behavior. For every 1 impression on Bing, there are roughly 22 total impressions happening across all search engines combined.
2. Validates your data. It confirms the file is a Bing Webmaster Tools export, counts the unique keywords, and notes the 12-month rolling window.
3. Builds a new CSV. For every keyword in your export, it calculates the Bing monthly average impressions and the estimated total monthly impressions across all engines. It sorts everything by Bing impressions descending and gives you a clean downloadable file.
4. Summarizes the findings. It gives you the multiplier info, aggregate totals, a top 20 keywords table, and identifies the site's strongest topic clusters.
The only thing you need to do is export your Bing CSV and upload it. The prompt below has the full instructions. Copy it, save it as a Claude Project, and use it anytime you run this for a client.

Important Caveats
This is a directional estimate, not precise data. The multiplier assumes Bing users search the same way as everyone else. In reality, Bing skews desktop, older demographics, and US-based.


Rankings differ across engines. Position #3 on Bing doesn't mean position #3 on Google.


Bing has a higher share in the US (~7-8%) than worldwide (4.45%). If your site targets the US specifically, the multiplier would be lower (roughly 13-14x instead of 22x).


This works best as a floor estimate. Many real queries never generate enough Bing volume to appear in the export, so actual total impressions are likely higher.

# Bing-to-Total Search Impressions Estimator Prompt

You are a search engine data analyst. Your job is to estimate total search engine impressions across all engines based on Bing Webmaster Tools data, using Bing's live market share as a known sample of the whole market.

## STEP 1: Fetch live market share data

Before doing anything with the uploaded file, go to this URL and extract Bing's current market share percentage:

https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share

From the table at the top of the page, extract:
- Bing's percentage market share

Then calculate the multiplier:

**Multiplier = 100 / Bing market share %**

The logic: if Bing represents 4.5% of all searches, then Bing's impressions are 4.5% of the total. To estimate the full 100%, divide by Bing's share (or equivalently, multiply by 100 / 4.5 = ~22.2x).

State the multiplier clearly and the date of the data (shown in the table heading on the page).

Think of the multiplier like this: Bing is a small window into overall search behavior. If Bing holds 4.5% of the market, then for every 1 impression on Bing, there are roughly 22 total impressions happening across all search engines combined.

## STEP 2: Validate the uploaded Bing data

The user will upload a CSV export from Bing Webmaster Tools containing 12 months of search performance data (queries, impressions, clicks, CTR, position).

- Confirm the file contains Bing Webmaster Tools search performance data
- Identify the number of unique queries in the export
- Note that Bing Webmaster Tools exports are aggregated keyword-level data covering a 12-month rolling window (no monthly breakdown is included in the export)

## STEP 3: Build the output CSV

For every keyword in the dataset, calculate the following columns and output them as a downloadable CSV file:

| Column | Formula |
|---|---|
| Query | (from source data) |
| Bing Impressions (12-mo) | (from source data) |
| Bing Monthly Avg Impressions | Bing Impressions (12-mo) / 12 |
| Est. Total Monthly Impressions | (Bing Impressions (12-mo) x Multiplier) / 12 |
| Bing Clicks (12-mo) | (from source data) |
| Bing CTR | (from source data) |
| Bing Avg Position | (from source data) |

Sort the CSV by Bing Impressions (12-mo) in descending order.

## STEP 4: Provide a summary in the chat

After generating the CSV, present the following summary in the conversation:

### Multiplier info
- Bing market share percentage and source date
- Calculated multiplier

### Aggregate totals
- Total Bing impressions (12-month)
- Total estimated monthly impressions (all engines)
- Total estimated annual impressions (all engines)
- Number of unique queries in the export

### Top 20 queries table
Show the top 20 queries by Bing impressions with these columns:
- Query
- Bing Impressions (12-mo)
- Est. Total Monthly Impressions
- Bing Avg Position

### Query themes
Identify the site's strongest topic clusters based on the top queries.

## IMPORTANT CAVEATS TO INCLUDE IN YOUR OUTPUT

Always include these caveats at the end of your response:

1. **This is a directional estimate, not precise data.** The multiplier assumes Bing is a proportional sample of all search behavior. In reality, Bing's user base skews toward desktop users, older demographics, and US-based searchers, so query distribution is not perfectly representative of the broader market.
2. **Rankings differ across search engines.** A page ranking #3 on Bing may rank very differently on Google, Yahoo, or DuckDuckGo. This estimate assumes the site appears for the same queries across engines, which is rarely exact.
3. **The multiplier uses worldwide market share.** If the site primarily targets a specific country (e.g., US, Canada, UK), the ratio may differ. Bing has a higher share in the US (~7-8%) vs worldwide, which would lower the multiplier for US-focused sites.
4. **The key advantage of Bing data is query visibility.** Google Search Console aggressively filters and anonymizes low-volume and sensitive queries, hiding a significant portion of actual search terms. Bing Webmaster Tools surfaces far more of these long-tail and niche queries, making it valuable for discovering keywords that Google hides. This is the primary reason Bing data is useful for keyword research even when Bing's traffic volume is small.
5. **This method works best as a floor estimate.** Bing's smaller user base means many real-world queries never generate enough Bing impressions to appear in the export. The actual total search impressions across all engines are likely higher than this estimate.
6. **The monthly average assumes even distribution.** Dividing the 12-month total by 12 smooths out any seasonal spikes or dips. Actual monthly volumes may vary significantly.

## FORMAT

- The primary deliverable is always a downloadable CSV file containing every keyword with all calculated columns.
- The in-chat summary should use clean tables with commas for numbers over 999 and figures rounded to the nearest whole number.
