## 星球正文

Your biggest SEO opportunity is your category pages:

Simone Parodi 在 LinkedIn 发的电商 SEO 清单，观点是：电商站最大的 SEO 机会在类目页（产品列表页），给了 11 条优化动作。

要点：
1. 内链：给新上的产品列表页加内链；用有针对性的内链给重点类目页加权重；在类目页上放图块式链接（tile links），作者说见效快。
2. 结构：做一套语义清晰的筛选（filter）；找那些对业务和 SEO 都有价值的新类目来开。
3. 页面本身：类目标题要贴合用户意图；在类目页底部加以用户为中心、带关键词的内容，去吃长尾词；加结构化数据（schema）；优化加载速度，减少跳出。
4. 持续跟进：定期看类目页表现，按用户行为和搜索趋势更新内容。

    Search is becoming more than just typing a keyword into Google.

    With multimodal search, shoppers can increasingly start their journey with an image, photo, or screenshot.

    The keyword with the most searches isn't always the keyword worth ranking for.

    This is one of the easiest mistakes to make in ecommerce SEO.

    A founder sees a keyword with huge volume and thinks:

    “We need to rank for that.”

    But search volume doesn't tell you how valuable the searcher is.

    Someone searching for general information might generate thousands of visits and almost no revenue.

    Someone searching for a very specific product category might generate far less traffic — but have a much stronger reason to buy.

    That's why I prefer starting with the commercial question:

    “If we ranked here, would this searcher actually be valuable to the business?”

    Then I look at the page that should capture that demand.

    Sometimes it's a collection.

    Sometimes it's a product page.

    Sometimes the site needs a better structure before either page has a real chance.

    SEO becomes much more interesting when you stop chasing traffic and start mapping search demand to revenue.

    The goal isn't to rank for more words.

    It's to become visible at the moments that matter.

    ◆ **How to Get Your Ecommerce Site Ranked High With SEO: Check Indexation Before Optimization**◆

    You cannot rank a page Google has not chosen to index.

    That sounds obvious.

    But it changes how I would diagnose**how to get your ecommerce site ranked high with seo**.

    When an important ecommerce page is not performing, adding more optimization is not necessarily the first move.

    First, I want to know:

    **Is Google actually able and willing to index the URL we want to rank?**Because ecommerce platforms can create competing technical signals at scale.

    A product or collection may exist perfectly well for shoppers while Google encounters several versions of essentially the same content through:

    → Faceted URLs
→ URL parameters
→ Duplicate or near-duplicate pages
→ Product variations
→ Alternative URL paths

    Now canonicalization becomes important.

    Suppose the page you want to rank points its canonical signal somewhere else.
Or Google identifies another URL as the better canonical.

    You could continue improving the intended page, but you may be strengthening a URL that Google has not selected for indexing.

    **▌**My diagnostic order would be different.

    Before asking,**“How can we improve this page?”**, I would investigate:

    **1. Index coverage**Is the intended URL indexed? If not, what reason is Google reporting?

    **2. Canonical signals**What canonical does the page declare—and which URL has Google selected?

    **3. Duplication**Are multiple URLs presenting substantially overlapping content?

    **4. Faceted URLs**Are filters generating large numbers of crawlable combinations that compete with cleaner URLs?

    **5. XML sitemaps**Are the URLs we actually want indexed being consistently surfaced through the sitemap?

    This is why technical diagnosis should come before repeated optimization.

    There are really two different problems:

    **Optimization problem:**Google has indexed the correct URL, but it is not performing strongly enough.

    **Indexation problem:**Google has not indexed—or has not selected—the URL you expect to compete.
Those problems require different responses.

    When investigating**how to get your ecommerce site ranked high with seo**, I would therefore establish the indexation state before making ranking recommendations.

    For ecommerce stores with thousands of products, collections, filters, and parameter combinations, this becomes even more important because duplication can scale automatically.

    **Optimization helps a page compete.** **Indexation determines whether that page can enter the competition in the first place.** If important ecommerce pages are struggling to rank, checking their indexation and canonical signals is a useful place to begin the technical investigation.
