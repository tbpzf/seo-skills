# Top 15 Indexing Myths of 2026

- [![Picture of David Quaid](http://primaryposition.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/cropped-king-of-seo-96x96.jpeg) David Quaid](https://primaryposition.com/blog/author/david-quaid/)
- [September 26, 2026](https://primaryposition.com/blog/2026/09/26/)

We have created a lot of content on how Crawling and Indexing works. Here’s a quick summary of Indexing myths

1. Most “indexing problems” are not technical errors. Google crawled the page and decided it was not worth storing.
2. Google is trying to save money
3. You can optimize crawl budget
4. Less URLs = more time crawling your other pages
5. More Crawling = more indexing or ranking
6. Crawling and indexing are separate jobs. Fetching a URL does not put it in the index.
7. “Crawled – currently not indexed” is usually an authority problem, not robots, noindex, 404s, 500s, or a bad canonical. Those have their own GSC labels.
8. “Discovered – currently not indexed” means Google knows the URL exists and has not bothered to fetch it yet. Same root cause, earlier stage.
9. More crawling does not mean more indexing. Sitemap pings and manual inspect requests do not force a slot in the index.
10. You cannot really “optimize crawl budget” the way people talk about it. Google recrawls important, authoritative pages often and ignores the rest.
11. There is no fixed crawl schedule. Strong pages may be hit in days. Weak pages may wait months.
12. A sitemap is a hint, not a command. Weak, thin, or duplicate URLs in XML still get skipped.
13. LLMs are not a second search engine with their own web-scale index. If traditional search will not store you, AI answers have less to cite.
14. Schema, llms.txt, and “chunking for AI” do not buy indexation. They do not replace links, relevance, or unique value.
15. Then do the unglamorous work: earn authority, link internally from pages that already rank, and make the URL add something the site does not already cover.
