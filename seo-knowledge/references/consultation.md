# Consultation and source use

## Select by the actual task

Use `scripts/search.rb` relative to the installed `seo-knowledge` directory.
`--route` supplies curated editorial leads; `--query` searches titles, tags,
star-post bodies, and curated subject terms. Query words are literal discovery
terms, not semantic proof of relevance. Search output gives source availability
and file/line locations; read the selected passages before adoption.
If Ruby is not available, read/search `guidance.json` for route/topic leads and
`library/catalog.json` for their filenames, capture status, and star/media/
original ranges; then inspect those bundled files using the host's normal file
tools. This is the same consultation without the optional command helper, not
a reason to install software or promote uninspected material to evidence.

For a non-SEO product article, search separately for its subject and for the
editorial decision (for example, evidence, audience, structure, or voice). When
no subject source fits, report that honestly and use the relevant writing
methods only. A post about writing better landing pages cannot establish a
photo product's features, conversion rate, or customers' actual objections.

Prefer the smallest sufficient source set. Route leads in
[writing-guidance.md](writing-guidance.md) help avoid installation tutorials,
account setup, analytics operations, or navigation text that merely mentions
writing. Those are not automatic writing prerequisites. If a technical subject
is the requested article's topic, its actual supporting content may be useful.

## Inspect the source, not its label

- Read the star body and the relevant captured original. The catalog's
  `captured` means a page was retrieved, not that its claims are verified or
  that the intended article is present. A directory listing, app welcome screen,
  video description, or publisher notice supports only what it actually says.
- `partial` supports its accessible excerpt only. `unavailable` supplies a
  missing-source lead, not original text. A post with no external link may be
  cited as the star author's statement, with that provenance and limit.
- Read any returned `media_lines` and open the local image links when they carry
  the substance or evidence. Image-only posts need those images, not just their
  placeholder text. An attached PDF or ZIP with `metadata_only` has no readable
  contents in this bundle; its filename proves neither a method nor product facts.
- Distinguish post date, original publication/update date, observation window,
  and archive capture time. An October snapshot of a May post may contain an
  updated original; it is not necessarily the May version.
- Several posts or tracking URLs repeating one original are not independent
  evidence. Conflicting dates, undisclosed methods, commercial incentives,
  sample limits, and changed claims remain visible in the consultation.

## Apply rather than accumulate

**Editorial guidance** changes how the work is planned, written, or checked:
reader situations, evidence collection, descriptive headings, bounded claims,
source placement, voice, or meaningful refreshes. Record the decision in the
existing plan/brief or findings. Do not insert irrelevant SEO commentary or
star links into a product article just to show that research happened.

**Factual evidence** supports a reader-facing claim. Check the exact supporting
passage, author/source, scope, date, and conflicts. The evidence packet retains
its existing `supplied`, `checked`, `hypothesis`, and `unknown` labels; retrieval
alone does not promote a claim to `checked`. Inspect current first-party sources
when a present-tense product fact, publisher policy, search rule, statistic, or
other material time-sensitive claim depends on them. Without access, preserve
the uncertainty and omit or narrow unsupported claims.

A host's current submission rules and disclosures control a guest post;
approved product facts control a landing page; the actual news event and
distributor rules control a press release. Current official search guidance
applies only to that engine and its stated domain. A secondary interpretation
is attributed to its author, not presented as a direct official statement.
Do not enforce observational word counts, image counts, freshness intervals,
link ratios, or model experiment results as universal SEO rules.

## Reuse and export

Keep one consultation in the parent's existing packet, plan, brief, submission
notes, or review report. Reuse it downstream when scope, sources, and facts fit;
record no-match/unavailable/deferred outcomes instead of silently dropping the
step. For new body copy, do only a bounded inventory before author intake;
expanded reading does not replace the author's source question.

A substantive revision refreshes affected guidance and evidence only. A local
wording/grammar correction preserves the current contract and does not trigger
a library survey. A read-only audit returns findings without edits or an
interview. Humanization preserves adopted decisions and source limits, and
returns new fact questions to the parent.

A standalone prompt includes useful selected guidance, original URLs, limits,
and enough source context to act without the archive. It may invite a future
writer with the installed library to consult it, but a local path is not the
only instruction or proof. A saved-plan export retains the parent's existing
consultation and unresolved gaps; it does not silently research or replan.
