# Reader brief for a guest post

Use this method during the angle step of the guest-post workflow. The target is
the host publication's reader in a specific situation, not a broad persona or a
keyword cluster. A product audience can overlap with that reader, but the host
still decides editorial fit.

## Build the brief

Record only the situation details that affect the article:

- Who is facing the problem, what triggered it, and what they already know
- What they are trying to do or decide now; what question follows next
- Constraints, concerns, and evidence they need to act
- Which details came from reader/customer evidence, the host's published work,
  or contributor expertise; label the rest `hypothesis` or `unknown`

Use the seven category-entry-point prompts when the situation is still vague:
why, when, where, while doing what, with whom, with or for what, and how the
reader feels. They are prompts for investigation, not seven mandatory fields or
facts to invent. Customer-facing teams, support questions, interviews, and the
host's reader comments can supply evidence. Respect the source's scope and
privacy when using internal material.

Place the article at the reader's current stage: exploring the problem,
checking a method or constraint, validating an approach, or taking a next
action. Use supplied or observed queries to learn the reader's language and
check for unanswered questions. Choose the angle for editorial usefulness and
supportable expertise, not search volume or a backlink target.

## Sources and adaptation

- Craig Addyman, [The Anti-Keyword Strategy](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/anti-keyword-strategy-craig-addyman-xmzce/): audience motivations and distinct search language, decision-stage mapping, and query data used to refine a strategy.
- Amanda King, [Build content briefs around people, not keywords](https://martech.org/build-content-briefs-around-people-not-keywords/): concrete reader situations, seven category-entry-point prompts, and evidence from customer-facing teams.

Both articles discuss owned-site content strategy. This brief transfers their
reader-first research method to a publisher-controlled article. Site hubs,
internal-link architecture, owned-page CTAs, and claims about AI search
visibility are not guest-post requirements or promised outcomes.
