# [5/6] SEO myth: Why some sites rank for almost anything

### The SEO strategy that looks smart but stalls growth

If clarity and consistency matter this much…

**why do some sites seem to rank for** ***everything*** **anyway?**

You know the ones.

They publish broadly.

They jump between topics.

And they still win.

Let’s clear this up, because this is where many strategies quietly go wrong.

### The illusion of ranking for everythingSVG Image

Most sites don’t actually rank for everything.

They rank for many things **from different roles**.

This distinction changes everything.

When Google evaluates content, it’s not asking:
> “Is this site allowed to talk about this topic?”

It’s asking:
> “From which role does this site speak most credibly here?”

Some sites are trusted to play *multiple roles*.

Most are not.

### Why big sites get away with itSVG Image

Sites that appear to rank for anything usually have one of two advantages:

### 1. They are trusted as a *meta-source*SVG Image

Think:

- Encyclopedias

- Aggregators

- Broad explainers

Their role is breadth itself.

Google expects them to cover many topics — and evaluates them accordingly.

### 2. They have multiple stable identitiesSVG Image

Large sites don’t rank broadly from one fuzzy identity.

They rank from **internally consistent clusters**:

- Clear sections

- Clear framing

- Clear intent boundaries

To Google, these behave almost like *separate sources* under one domain.

This takes years and extreme discipline.

### Why copying this usually failsSVG Image

Here’s the trap:

A focused site sees a broad site ranking widely and thinks:
> “We should do that too.”

But without:

- A meta-role

- Or multiple stable clusters

What happens instead is **context collapse**.

Google doesn’t see versatility.

It sees uncertainty.

And uncertainty leads to:

- Lower trust ceilings

- Slower growth

- Isolated wins that don’t compound

### Specialists don’t lose because they’re narrowSVG Image

They lose when they’re **unclear**.

A specialist with a sharp role will often outrank a generalist *within the right kind of query*.

The mistake isn’t choosing focus.

The mistake is abandoning it.

### The strategic choice (most teams avoid making)SVG Image

Every content strategy eventually faces this fork:

**Option A:**

Win a clear role

→ Compound trust

→ Predictable growth

**Option B:**

Chase topics

→ Diffuse signals

→ Flat curve disguised as activity

Big sites can afford Option B *after* winning Option A.

Most others can’t.

### The real takeawaySVG Image

This isn’t about playing small.

It’s about deciding:
> “What kind of source do we want to be known as?”

Once that’s clear, topic selection becomes easier.

Framing becomes sharper.

And Google’s confidence compounds instead of resetting.

Which leads to the final question:

If role clarity matters this much…

**how do you design for it intentionally instead of drifting into it?**

That’s where we’ll end this series.
