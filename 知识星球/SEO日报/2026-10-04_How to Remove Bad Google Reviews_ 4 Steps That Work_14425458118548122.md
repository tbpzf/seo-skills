How to Remove Bad Google Reviews: 4 Steps That Work

Google 商家资料（Google Business Profile）上收到差评怎么删？本地 SEO 公司 Sterling Sky 讲得很直白：Google 只删违反它内容政策的评论，单纯"差评""不公平""跟客户有纠纷"都不会删。真要删，得按顺序走四步。

要点：
1. 能删的类型：假评论（没有真实消费经历）、拿钱或拿好处换来的评论、一个人用多个账号刷的、竞争对手刷的、有利益冲突的（比如在职或离职员工）、跟这家店无关的、含骚扰威胁或个人信息的、刻意刷分的异常评论。举报前先想清楚它违反的是哪一条。
2. 四步流程：在商家资料里点评论旁边的三个点举报 → 用 Google 的 Reviews Management Tool 查处理状态（Decision pending 是还没判、Report reviewed – no policy violation 是判了没违规、Escalated 是申诉已升级）→ 判没违规的可以申诉，只有一次机会，一次最多选 10 条，写清楚违反了哪条政策并附证据，记下 case ID → 还不行就带着 case ID 去 Google Business Profile 社区找产品专家帮忙。
3. Google 说评估一般要几天，但作者见过长期卡在 Decision pending 的；这时别反复举报同一条，去看能不能申诉，或者联系支持。
4. 如果有人刷差评再找你要钱才肯删，别付钱，保存聊天记录、评论链接、对方信息和时间线，走 Google 专门的商家勒索举报通道。反过来，商家自己也不能给顾客好处让他改评或删评，这本身就违规。

结论：没违反政策的真实差评，好好回复、解决顾客的问题，比反复举报更有用；也要小心那些保证能帮你删差评的公司。

Getting a negative review on your *Google Business Profile*, previously known as *Google My Business*, can be one of the most frustrating parts of being a business owner.

Understanding how to remove bad reviews from Google is important for protecting your business’s online reputation. However, Google will only remove reviews that violate its policies, not reviews simply because they are negative or because a business disagrees with them.

### TLDR:

- **Flag the Review:** Find the review on your Google Business Profile, click the three-dot menu, and report it if you believe it violates Google’s policies.

- **Check the Review Status:** Use Google’s Reviews Management Tool to monitor the reported review. Google says review evaluation typically takes several days.

- **Appeal the Decision:** If Google determines there was no policy violation, eligible reviews can receive a one-time appeal. Clearly explain which policy the review violates.

- **Get Additional Help:** If your appeal fails and you still believe the review clearly violates Google’s policies, you can seek assistance through the Google Business Profile Community.

- **Report Review Extortion:** If someone demands money, goods, or services in exchange for removing negative reviews, don’t pay them. Google has a separate process for reporting negative review extortion scams.

## What Types of Google Reviews Can Be Removed?

Google does not **remove a bad Google review** simply because it is negative or because a business disagrees with what the reviewer said. To qualify for **Google review removal**, a review generally needs to violate [Google’s content policies](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114).

Examples of Google reviews that may qualify for removal include:

- **Fake Google reviews** that are not based on a genuine experience with the business.

- **Paid or incentivized reviews**, including reviews posted in exchange for money, discounts, free products, or services.

- **Reviews posted from multiple accounts** by or at the request of one person.

- **Competitor reviews** posted to damage another business’s reputation.

- **Reviews involving a conflict of interest**, which can include current or former employees and certain professional, contractual, family, or personal relationships.

- **Off-topic reviews** that do not describe an experience with the business.

- **Reviews containing prohibited harassment, threats, personal information, or other restricted content.**

- **Unusual patterns of reviews** intended to manipulate a business’s rating.

Not sure whether a review qualifies for removal? Our guide to [Google review policy violations](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-review-policy-violations/) explains the types of reviews we’ve seen Google remove and the ones Google typically leaves up.  

Before reporting a negative Google review, identify the specific Google policy you believe the review violates. This is particularly important if Google does not initially remove the review and you need to submit a **Google review appeal**.

For a deeper look at which reviews violate Google’s rules, see our guide to **Google review policy violations**.

## Four Steps to Get Negative Reviews Removed From Google

There are four main steps for requesting the removal of a policy-violating review from your Google Business Profile. It’s important to follow the process in the correct order.

## Step 1: Flag the Review in Your Google Business Profile

The first step to **remove a negative Google review** is to report it to Google.

Go to your Google Business Profile and select **Read reviews**. Find the review you believe violates Google’s policies, click the three-dot menu next to the review, and select the option to report it.

Google will ask you to select the reason you believe the review should be removed, such as spam or profanity. Select the policy violation that most accurately describes the problem and submit the report.

Fun fact: That three-dot menu is sometimes called a “kebab menu.”

### google business profile kebab menu

 

The limitation of initially flagging a negative review is that you don’t have much room to provide Google with additional context. You are essentially telling Google that you believe the review violates a policy and asking Google to evaluate it.

This is why identifying the correct policy violation before reporting the review is important.

## Step 2: Check the Status of Your Google Review

After you report a Google review for removal, you can monitor its status using Google’s Reviews Management Tool. Google says review evaluation typically takes several days, although some cases can take longer.

If the review has not yet been evaluated, you may see the status **“Decision pending.”** A **Google review decision pending** status means the review has been flagged for removal, but Google has not yet made a decision about whether it violates its policies.

To check the status of a review you reported:

1. Open [Google’s Reviews Management Tool](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773).

2. Confirm that you are signed in with the Google account associated with the Business Profile.

3. Select the correct business.

4. Choose the option to check the status of a review you previously reported.

5. Locate the review and check its current status.

The Reviews Management Tool may display statuses such as:

- **Decision pending:** The review has been reported but has not yet been evaluated.

- **Report reviewed – no policy violation:** Google evaluated the review and did not identify a policy violation. You may be eligible to submit a one-time appeal.

- **Escalated – check your email for updates:** Your appeal has been escalated, and Google will send the final decision by email.

If Google determines that the review violates its policies, the review may be removed from Google Search and Maps.

## What to Do If a Google Review Is Stuck in Decision Pending

If you have a **decision pending Google review** that remains in that status for an extended period, it means Google has not yet completed its evaluation of the reported review. Although Google says review evaluations typically take several days, we have seen cases where reviews remain in **Decision Pending** much longer.

If your **Google review decision is pending**, here’s what to do:

### 1. Check the Reviews Management Tool

Return to Google’s Reviews Management Tool and check whether the review is still listed as **Decision Pending** or has become eligible for an appeal.

If the review is eligible for an appeal, use the appeal option provided in the tool rather than repeatedly reporting the same review.

### 2. Gather Evidence That the Review Violates Google’s Policies

Before escalating the issue, document exactly why you believe the review violates Google’s policies.

For example, if you believe it is a **fake Google review**, gather evidence supporting your position. If several suspicious reviews appear connected to the same person or incident, document those connections.

Useful evidence may include screenshots of the review, the reviewer’s name and profile, direct links to the review, relevant business records, evidence connecting multiple suspicious reviews, and emails or other relevant communications.

### 3. Appeal the Google Review If Eligible

If the Reviews Management Tool allows you to appeal the review, submit the appeal and clearly explain which Google policy the review violates.

Be specific. Instead of simply stating, “This is a fake review,” explain why you believe it violates Google’s fake engagement or other applicable policy.

### 4. Contact Google Business Profile Support If Necessary

If the review remains stuck in **Decision Pending** for an unusually long period and the Reviews Management Tool does not provide an appeal option, you may need to contact Google Business Profile Support.

Provide the Business Profile information, review URL, screenshots, and any existing support case numbers.

### 5. Use the Google Business Profile Community

If you’ve exhausted the available review-removal and appeal options and still believe the review clearly violates Google’s policies, you can post your case in the Google Business Profile Community.

Include your case ID when available, explain which policy you believe the review violates, and provide enough context for Product Experts to understand the situation. Avoid publicly sharing sensitive customer or business information.

We’ve seen cases where businesses had a **Google review stuck in Decision Pending** for an extended period and were eventually able to get the issue reviewed after escalating it with clear documentation.

The key is to follow Google’s review-removal process in order, document the policy violation, and use the available appeal and support channels rather than repeatedly reporting the same review.

## What to Do If Someone Is Extorting You With Negative Google Reviews

If your business receives suspicious negative Google reviews followed by demands for money, goods, or services in exchange for removing them, you may be dealing with a **Google review extortion scam**.

Google has a dedicated process for reporting negative review extortion. This is different from the standard process used to report a fake or policy-violating Google review.

If you believe your Google Business Profile is being targeted by review extortion:

1. **Do not pay or engage with the person making the demand.** Paying does not guarantee that the negative reviews will be removed and may encourage additional attempts.

2. **Gather evidence immediately.** Save screenshots of emails, text messages, WhatsApp messages, social media messages, or other communications containing the demands.

3. **Save direct links to the suspicious Google reviews.**

4. **Document information about the people involved**, including names, usernames, email addresses, phone numbers, and social media profiles.

5. **Record the timeline**, including when the negative reviews appeared and when you received the demand.

6. **Submit the evidence through [Google’s merchant extortion reporting process](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16404809).**

This special reporting process is intended specifically for situations involving demands for money, goods, or favors in exchange for removing negative Google reviews. Fake, spam, off-topic, or otherwise policy-violating reviews that do not involve extortion should continue to be reported through Google’s standard review-removal process.  

We’ve also documented[how Google review extortion scams work and how businesses can fight back](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-business-review-extortion-scam-how-to-fight-back/), including common patterns we’ve seen in fake review attacks.

### Don’t Pay Someone to Remove a Negative Google Review

**Important:** Don’t offer customers money, discounts, free products, or services in exchange for changing or removing a negative Google review. Google prohibits incentivized review revision or removal as a form of rating manipulation.

There is an important distinction between legitimately resolving a customer’s complaint and offering compensation on the condition that the customer removes or changes their Google review.  

Businesses should also avoid [review gating and other methods of selectively soliciting positive reviews](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/review-gating-is-now-against-the-google-my-business-guidelines/), which can violate Google’s review policies and result in reviews being removed.

## Step 3: How to Appeal a Google Review Removal Decision

If Google determines that a reported review does not violate its policies, you may be eligible to submit a **Google review appeal** through the Reviews Management Tool.

The appeal is your opportunity to explain why the negative review should be removed and identify the specific Google policy you believe it violates.

Google currently allows you to select up to **10 eligible reviews** when submitting an appeal.

When writing your appeal, don’t simply say that a review is fake, unfair, or inaccurate. Explain specifically why it violates Google’s policies.

For example, if a former employee posted a negative review about your business, explain the conflict of interest and identify the policy that applies. If you believe the review is not based on a genuine experience, explain why and provide whatever relevant evidence you have.

Google allows only **one appeal** of a review-removal decision, so make your appeal as specific and complete as possible.

After submitting the appeal, save the case ID Google provides. You may need this case ID if you later seek additional assistance through Google Business Profile Support or the Google Business Profile Community.

## Step 4: Get Help From the Google Business Profile Community

If your **Google review appeal** is denied and you still believe the review clearly violates Google’s policies, you can post your case in the Google Business Profile Community.

Product Experts and other experienced community members may be able to review the circumstances and, in appropriate cases, help escalate an issue to Google.

When posting your case:

- Include your Google review appeal case ID.

- Explain which Google policy you believe the review violates.

- Provide clear supporting evidence.

- Explain the steps you’ve already taken to remove the review.

- Avoid posting private or sensitive customer information publicly.

Keep in mind that a negative review will not be removed simply because a business believes it is unfair. The review must violate Google’s policies.

## What Google Reviews Cannot Be Removed?

Google generally will not remove a review simply because:

- The review is negative.

- The business disagrees with the customer’s version of events.

- The business believes the review is unfair.

- The customer and business are involved in a legitimate dispute.

- The business does not like the star rating.

Google does not act as an arbitrator in ordinary disputes between businesses and customers. A negative review generally needs to violate Google’s content policies to qualify for removal.

If a legitimate negative Google review does not violate Google’s policies, [responding professionally to the Google review](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/how-to-respond-to-google-reviews/) and addressing the customer’s concerns is usually a better strategy than repeatedly reporting it.

You should also be cautious of reputation management companies that guarantee they can make negative content disappear. We’ve investigated [**how shady companies remove bad reviews from Google and why businesses should avoid these tactics**](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/how-companies-remove-bad-reviews-from-google/).

## Google Review Removal FAQs

### Can You Remove a Bad Google Review?

Yes, but only if the review violates Google’s content policies. Google will not remove a review simply because it is negative, unfair, or because the business disagrees with it. Fake reviews, spam, conflicts of interest, harassment, and other prohibited content may qualify for removal.

### How Do You Remove a Negative Google Review?

To request removal of a negative Google review, report the review through your Google Business Profile or Google’s Reviews Management Tool. Identify the policy the review violates, monitor Google’s decision, and submit a one-time appeal if the review is eligible and Google initially declines to remove it.

### What Does Decision Pending Mean on a Google Review?

A **Decision Pending Google review** means you have reported the review, but Google has not yet completed its evaluation. The review may remain visible while Google determines whether it violates its policies.

### Why Is My Google Review Decision Pending?

A **Google review decision pending** status means Google has received the report but has not made a final decision. Google says review evaluations typically take several days, but some reviews can remain pending longer. Check the Reviews Management Tool to determine whether the review is still pending or has become eligible for an appeal.

### What Should I Do If a Google Review Is Stuck in Decision Pending?

If a Google review remains stuck in **Decision Pending**, check the Reviews Management Tool to see whether it is eligible for appeal. If it is, submit an appeal explaining the specific policy violation. If no appeal option becomes available after an extended period, consider contacting Google Business Profile Support or seeking help through the Google Business Profile Community.

### Can You Appeal Google’s Decision Not to Remove a Review?

Yes. If Google determines that a reported review does not violate its policies, eligible reviews can receive a one-time appeal through the Reviews Management Tool. Explain exactly which policy the review violates.

### How Many Google Reviews Can You Appeal at Once?

Google currently allows businesses to select up to 10 eligible reviews when submitting a review-removal appeal.

### Can You Remove a Fake Google Review?

You can report a fake Google review for removal if it violates Google’s policies. When appealing a decision, clearly explain why you believe the review is not based on a genuine experience and identify the applicable Google policy.

### Can You Pay Someone to Remove a Negative Google Review?

You should not offer money, discounts, free goods, or services in exchange for someone changing or removing a negative Google review. Google prohibits incentivized review revision or removal as rating manipulation.

### What Should I Do If Someone Threatens My Business With Negative Google Reviews Unless I Pay Them?

Do not pay or engage with the person making the demand. Preserve screenshots, messages, review links, contact information, and other evidence, then use Google’s dedicated process for reporting negative review extortion.
