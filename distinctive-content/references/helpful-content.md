# Helpful content standard

The purpose of these workflows is to help a specific reader accomplish a real
task with trustworthy information and useful judgment. Search optimization
helps the right reader find that work. Editorial quality comes from the answer,
its evidence, and what the reader can do with it.

## Plan the answer

Record the reader's question, current knowledge, constraints, and a completion
signal: what they should be able to decide, do, or understand after reading.
Name the expected answer form, such as a procedure, worked example, checklist,
explanation, or buying criteria. An intent label alone is insufficient.

Use inspected audience material, supplied query data, and relevant search
results to support the interpretation. Record competing interpretations and
material uncertainty. Search results show existing expectations; customer
evidence helps explain the underlying need. Neither automatically proves the
other. When a commercial page cannot truthfully satisfy the supplied query,
surface the mismatch before drafting. When research is unavailable, label the
interpretation as a hypothesis and keep the promise within known facts.

For a guest article, also satisfy the host's reader and editorial purpose. For
a press release, the task belongs to a journalist or stakeholder evaluating a
real news event; an SEO query is optional.

## Earn the contribution

State the baseline answer and what this piece adds: a more usable method,
reasoned decision rule, worked example, clearer synthesis, original observation,
product demonstration, or a consequential limit. Explain why that addition
matters to this reader. A unique opinion is valuable when its reasoning helps
the decision; a contrarian claim alone adds nothing.

For a recommendation or viewpoint, show the chain from checked facts or
attributed experience to reasoning, recommendation, alternatives, and the
conditions under which the recommendation changes. Label an editorial
synthesis as analysis. Treat a constructed example as illustrative, never as a
customer case or measured result. Useful synthesis can qualify without original
data or first-hand experience. Claims that a contribution is absent from other
pages require an actual comparison; otherwise describe its value without a
novelty claim.

Support the central promise as a whole. One sourced fact or product limitation
cannot validate a complete how-to or broad outcome claim. Each major section
needs a concrete reader job and answer, with evidence where the claim requires
it. Use mapped distinctive material where useful; ordinary explanation and
necessary context may need no unique source.

## Keep trust visible

Distinguish supplied statements, checked sources, hypotheses, and unknowns.
Inspect a source before calling a claim checked; a citation by itself is not
verification. Keep material claims tied to the supporting passage or artifact,
relevant scope or date, and limits. Documentation supports described product
behavior; customer outcomes and comparisons require their own evidence.
Attribution, quotes, experience, and results must match the source and approval.
Research and reasoning may supply an answer; they cannot create an author's
experience or credentials.

Safe omissions and a narrower truthful promise can resolve optional gaps.
An indispensable missing fact, unsupported central answer, or unresolved
factual conflict blocks body drafting when no defensible answer remains. Ask
the user only for essential knowledge the agent cannot obtain. Carry any
pending question and interview count through the parent plan or guest notes.
Editorial placeholders and proof requests belong in review notes, outside
publishable copy.

## Verify the finished work

Run a skeptical reader walkthrough against the final copy, after editing:

1. Find the answer promised by the title, opening, and reader brief.
2. Try to perform the procedure, apply the criteria, inspect the example, or
   follow the explanation. Identify any missing input, choice, step, result,
   or exception that would prevent the intended task.
3. Check the evidence and reasoning behind the contribution. Name what it adds
   beyond the baseline answer and where its recommendation stops applying.
4. Check that necessary context, alternatives, and limits survived rewrites;
   remove sections that add no answer, action, or decision support.
5. Record the result as `pass`, `repair needed`, or `blocked`, with the exact
   passage, unmet reader need, and repair. Repair local defects and rerun the
   affected check. A material unresolved defect blocks publication readiness.

Keep an unavailable comparison or search observation explicit; never convert
`unknown` into a successful check. An optional research gap need not block an
otherwise supported answer. Workflow execution and publication readiness are
separate decisions. Word counts, readability grades, keyword counts, prose
scores, and completed editing stages cannot establish helpfulness.

## Basis

[Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
asks about original analysis, added value beyond rewriting sources, trust,
and whether readers learn enough to achieve their goal. It also rejects a
preferred word count. These editorial tests apply those ideas; they are not a
ranking formula, certification, or claim that a particular format ranks.
