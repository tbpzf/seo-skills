# Research and judgment

Use this branch when inspected public material, supplied artifacts, or reasoned
synthesis can strengthen the contribution. Apply the
[helpful-content standard](helpful-content.md).

## Fill accessible gaps

1. Read relevant supplied sources or artifacts. If needed and tools are
   available, research the smallest set of primary documentation, original
   research, or reputable original reporting that can resolve the gap. A search
   snippet or an unvisited URL is a lead, not checked evidence.
2. Record the supporting passage, artifact, or data reference with its owner,
   applicable scope, and date/version when freshness matters. Distinguish what
   the source establishes from what remains inferred. Product documentation
   establishes behavior; customer outcomes need outcome evidence.
3. Check relevant conflicts, applicability, and independence. Several summaries
   repeating one study do not constitute several independent sources. Preserve
   contradictory evidence that changes the recommendation.
4. If inspection is unavailable, keep the item `supplied`, `hypothesis`, or
   `unknown` as appropriate. Record the concrete gap; interview only if essential
   knowledge belongs to the user. Omit optional unsupported claims.

The branch is complete when each central claim has sufficient support or a
named blocker, and source checks can be traced from the packet. An inaccessible
source does not become `checked` through repetition or a remembered URL.

## Derive a judgment

Build the reasoning as `facts → reasoning → recommendation → limits`:

- Name the verified facts or explicit assumptions that matter to the reader's
  decision; keep inference separate from observations.
- Explain why those facts favor the proposed rule or recommendation over a
  relevant alternative. Cite the evidence; label the resulting conclusion as
  editorial analysis unless it is an attributed source's view.
- State the conditions where the rule applies and a counterexample or condition
  where the advice changes. Scale the claim to the evidence.

A useful contribution can be a clearer explanation, a worked example, synthesis
of evidence, a usable artifact, or a decision rule. Record the baseline answer
and how the contribution improves the reader's task. Claim that it is new or
absent from other coverage only after inspecting the relevant comparison.

Constructed examples must be labeled illustrative and grounded in checked
behavior or explicit assumptions. Tested examples require an actual recorded
test. Neither creates a customer story, personal experience, quote, or expert
endorsement. The judgment is complete when its reasoning is inspectable and
its scope makes clear when a reader should choose differently.
