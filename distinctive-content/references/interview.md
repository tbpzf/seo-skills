# Source interview patterns

Use these prompts for the default author intake before new body copy, and for
essential private knowledge on other routes. A brief source inventory makes
the question specific; extensive research waits until intake is resolved.
They are priorities, not a script. Ask one
question per turn and adapt it to the previous answer, the promised task, and
the highest-value remaining gap. Apply the
[helpful-content standard](helpful-content.md).

## First question by route

Ask for knowledge beyond the available feature descriptions or research. For
example, after inspecting a floor-plan editor's documented workflow, ask:

> 你做或测试这个 Floor Plan Editor 时，哪个具体修改场景最能体现它的价值？

Use the answer to choose the next gap rather than listing follow-up questions
in the same turn. A blog or guest post may start with a concrete situation the
author encountered; a press release may ask what changed for a user or why the
team made the announced decision. Ask in the user's conversational language;
the finished copy may still be English.

## Follow-up priorities

1. **Situation:** What specific situation led you or the team to work on this?
2. **Action:** What did you actually do, step by step, that a reader could
   reproduce or inspect?
3. **Decision:** Which choice mattered most, and what alternative did you
   reject?
4. **Constraint:** What limitation, edge case, cost, or risk changed the plan?
5. **Evidence:** What result, observation, measurement, source, or artifact
   supports the lesson? How was it measured?
6. **Failure:** What did not work, and how did you notice or correct it?
7. **Example:** Can you share a representative input, output, before/after, or
   anonymized case?
8. **Rule:** What rule of thumb would you use next time, and when would it fail?

Use an attribution question when the material will appear as a first-person
account, named expert view, quote, customer result, or company claim and its
source, preferred attribution, or necessary approval is unresolved. Reuse
recorded attribution and authorization instead of asking again.

Preserve the pending question, its gap, and the asked/answered/remaining count
in the packet. Count a question when it is presented to the user; a future
question stored in a plan-only artifact has not been asked. Count each asked
question once; resuming the saved question does
not start a new ten-question budget. Record an unanswered item as `unknown`.

Close author intake when the supplied or elicited material contributes a usable
experience, judgment, or example. Record the source and planned use, then
resolve accessible factual gaps through research. If the user reports no relevant
first-hand material, close intake with that answer and develop the contribution
through checked research or transparent analysis. An explicit request to skip
questions records `skipped by user`. A keyword-only or feature-only request
has not supplied author intake, even if all product facts can be researched.

Safely omit optional
gaps and return `provisional`; after the tenth question, return `blocked` if
essential support remains missing. Name the needed source or narrower viable
promise. The ceiling is not a target and does not authorize a generic draft.
